Meditation Techniques

An educational guide to distinct meditative traditions and practices, explained without jargon or religious affiliation to help you discover what feels most supportive.

Open Awareness

Mindfulness Meditation

What it means: Observing whatever unfolds in immediate experience without trying to hold onto it or push it away.

How it is practised: Sitting comfortably, noticing sensory phenomena, thoughts, and emotions like passing weather.

Beginner explanation: Picture yourself seated on a riverbank, gently watching leaves drift downstream rather than jumping in.

Suggested Length: 15 – 30 min Try Technique
Clear Seeing

Vipassana-Inspired Practice

What it means: Clear, discerning insight into the transient, impermanent nature of physical and mental sensations.

How it is practised: Systematically scanning somatic sensations across the physical form with steady, non-reactive equanimity.

Beginner explanation: Observing sensations such as warmth, pressure, or itchiness without reacting, learning they peak and dissolve.

Suggested Length: 30 – 45 min Try Technique
Concentration

Focused Attention

What it means: Narrowing the lens of cognitive attention onto a chosen focal object, such as breath sensation or a candle flame.

How it is practised: Sustaining attention on the single anchor. When you notice mind-wandering, calmly return without self-rebuke.

Beginner explanation: Like exercising a muscle, every return to the anchor strengthens attentional stability.

Suggested Length: 10 – 20 min Try Technique
Heart Centered

Loving-Kindness (Metta)

What it means: Actively fostering benevolence, patience, and non-judgmental friendliness toward yourself and others.

How it is practised: Silently reciting compassionate wishes ("May you be safe, may you be peaceful, may you live with ease").

Beginner explanation: Priming your emotional state to replace harsh inner criticism with genuine, grounded kindness.

Suggested Length: 15 – 30 min Try Technique
Sound Repetition

Mantra Meditation

What it means: Using a repetitive syllable, comforting word, or rhythmic phrase as an auditory stabilizing anchor.

How it is practised: Silently repeating a chosen calming phrase (e.g., "Peace", "Stillness", or "Present") in sync with breath.

Beginner explanation: Giving your vocal inner chatter a neutral, rhythmic word to repeat so the mind stays collected.

Suggested Length: 15 – 25 min Try Technique
Kinesthetic

Walking Meditation

What it means: Mindfulness practiced while moving slowly, turning everyday locomotion into a focused meditation.

How it is practised: Walking deliberately along an uninterrupted path, feeling foot soles lift, shift, and press into the earth.

Beginner explanation: An ideal option when seated sitting feels overly restless or brings physical discomfort.

Suggested Length: 15 – 30 min Try Technique
Somatic

Body Scan Meditation

What it means: Systematic exploration of bodily sensations, region by region, to bring attention into physical reality.

How it is practised: Moving attentional focus slowly from feet to legs, torso, chest, hands, neck, and face without trying to change what you feel.

Beginner explanation: A gentle internal check-in that identifies where you hold tension and invites natural release.

Suggested Length: 20 – 40 min Try Technique
Pranayama / Breath

Breath Awareness

What it means: Resting attention on the natural, spontaneous sensations of inhaling and exhaling.

How it is practised: Noticing the expansion of the ribs, the rising belly, or the slight breeze of air at the upper lip.

Beginner explanation: You do not need to alter how you breathe; simply observe breathing as it happens on its own.

Suggested Length: 10 – 25 min Try Technique
Pure Stillness

Silent Meditation

What it means: The practice of sitting in complete external quietude without speech, music, or guided verbal direction.

How it is practised: Taking a seated posture and maintaining self-sufficient mindfulness of mind and body for the duration.

Beginner explanation: Pure stillness where you become fully familiar with the natural textures of your own mind.

Suggested Length: 20 – 45 min Try Technique
